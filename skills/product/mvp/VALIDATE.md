# Validating on a real person

You have an MVP — something that reaches the aha. The goal is a real
person's verdict, not your own. Apply the rules in order.

## 1. Put it in front of someone real

Hand the MVP to a classmate, a friend, anyone who isn't you. Your own
excitement is not evidence. The only test is whether a real person reaches
the aha.

## 2. Watch, don't demo

Let them drive. Don't explain the thing first or click for them — if you
have to narrate, the journey is broken. Their unguided path to the aha is
the data.

## 3. Time it to the aha

Watch the clock. Did they reach the five-minute aha, or stall somewhere?
Where they stall is the part of the journey to cut or fix.

## 4. Trust what they do, not what they say

People are polite. The real signal is behavior: did they keep using it, ask
to use it again, or hand it back and change the subject? Words say
"interesting"; use says "valuable."

## 5. Act on the verdict

Either they hit the aha and you build the next feature — or they didn't, and
you change the journey and try again. Validation isn't the end of the loop;
it's the reset.
