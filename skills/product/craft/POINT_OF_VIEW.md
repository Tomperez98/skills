# Defining the point of view

You're about to build, or the thing you built has no character. The goal is
a point of view — what this is for you, for your users, who you want to be
to them — before the work starts. Apply the rules in order.

## 1. Define the point of view before you build

If you don't have a point of view, AI will give you one — and it will be
generic, looking at the past: the most probable answer, what's popular, what
was in style. So name it first: what is this product for you, what is it for
your users, who do you want to be to them, and what do they care about. That
forms the basis of your standards.

## 2. Write it down as standards

A point of view only scales if it's written. Every detail — the colors, the
way you write, what you write about, what you ship — flows from the point of
view. When building is distributed and ownership is diffuse, an unstated
point of view is no point of view at all: everyone fills the blank with
their own guess.

## 3. Judge the output, not the method

"Should something made with AI be held to a different standard?" is the
wrong question. Users don't care how it was made; they care whether it's
good. The bar is the output. If a discussion about how it was produced is
slowing you down, refocus on what you're actually striving for, then list
the concrete improvements.

## 4. Get good at noticing

Develop the point of view by noticing. Notice what your users need and want,
not just what they say. Notice what signals good, great, and meh — in the
products you use, and in analogous situations, so you can be inspired by
art, science, and a broader frame. Building your sense of taste raises the
standards you'll later encode and scale.

## 5. Go one level deeper than the customer can see

You aren't shooting for perfection — you're shooting one level past what
your customer would notice. That meticulous craft is invisible by
definition, but it shows up for them as a product that feels cared for.
