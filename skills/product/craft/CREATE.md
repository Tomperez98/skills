# Unleashing creativity and artistry

You have standards and an editor; now you want the thing to stand out and
feel alive. The goal is to use AI as a creative catalyst, not a copy
machine. Apply the rules in order.

## 1. Raise the ceiling, not just the floor

AI can help you manufacture monotony, but it's also the greatest creative
catalyst we've had. If you only use it to make what you already make, just
faster, you miss the most interesting part. AI lowers the cost to create —
spend some of that savings on making something truly special.

## 2. Improve your inputs

More specificity, in the direction of your brand and your unique interests.
Don't say "a website for my Korean barbecue" — say what you believe in, what
good is, what you care about. Feed it the source material you use to build
your own standards. Help it think in different ways. The output is only as
specific as the input.

## 3. Stress your outputs

Don't take the first result. It's the microwave-burrito dilemma: enamored by
the speed, you overlook that it's nearly inedible. Always push a step
further. Use adversarial agents to critique and bang it up a little — but
you yourself should always be pushing for better.

## 4. Invent new interfaces and aesthetics

Chats, charts, and CLIs are not the epitome of interaction in the modern
era. There is so much more to do to make things feel generative, alive,
responsive, dynamic. The best practices haven't been written yet — that's
the invitation. Like multi-touch or the synthesizer, AI opens whole new
possibility spaces. Branch out.

## 5. Protect the strange

Cookie-cutter patterns are safe and cozy; finding something unique that
improves the status quo is harder and more impressive. If you lead a team,
don't just tell people to use AI — give them room to explore. The strange
idea is often the differentiated one.
